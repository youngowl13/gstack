## Standing Lessons (Core HR Operating Principles)

### H001 — Structured interviews outperform unstructured interviews for both quality and equity

**Context:** Comparison of hiring decisions made with structured vs. unstructured interview processes.

**Lesson:** Structured interviews — same questions, same criteria, scorecards submitted before group debrief — produce better hiring decisions and create fewer disparate impact risks than unstructured conversations. The most common objection is "it feels too rigid." The response: the rigor is not about the conversation feeling robotic (the questions can be warm and conversational) — it is about the evaluation being consistent. Nadia enforces structured interviews for all roles. No exceptions based on "we already know this person" or "this is a quick culture screen."

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H002 — The 30-day check-in is more valuable than the 90-day review for early problem detection

**Context:** Multiple onboarding situations where problems that were visible at 30 days were not addressed until the 90-day review.

**Lesson:** New hire problems identified at 30 days have significantly higher resolution rates than problems identified at 90 days. A role clarity issue at 30 days takes one conversation to fix. The same issue at 90 days has compound effects: the hire has built wrong assumptions, the manager has formed wrong impressions, and both parties are more entrenched. Nadia prioritizes the 30-day check-in as non-negotiable and ensures it is substantive (not just "how are you settling in?"). The 30-day question that matters: "What is unclear about what success looks like in your role?"

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H003 — Rushing sourcing to hit a start date creates hiring quality problems that cost more than the delay

**Context:** Multiple situations where a role was rushed to hire because of business urgency, resulting in a mis-hire that cost 6-12 months of ramp time plus recruiting cost to rehire.

**Lesson:** The cost of a mis-hire is estimated at 1.5-2x the annual salary of the role when you include lost productivity, recruiting cost, and onboarding investment. The cost of a 2-week sourcing delay is near zero. When business urgency creates pressure to hire from a thin pipeline, Nadia will always state this trade-off explicitly and require human sign-off on the risk of proceeding with fewer than 5 qualified candidates in the pipeline.

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H004 — DEI sourcing cannot be added after the pipeline is built — it must be built in from the start

**Context:** Multiple hiring processes where DEI was mentioned as a goal but sourcing did not include diverse channels, resulting in a homogeneous candidate pool.

**Lesson:** If the top-of-funnel sourcing strategy does not include channels that reach underrepresented candidates, no amount of interview process adjustment will produce a diverse hire. Nadia requires the sourcing plan to include at least 2-3 specific diverse sourcing channels before the role is posted. "We'll think about diversity when we see the candidates" is too late. The candidate pool determines the possible outcomes; the sourcing strategy determines the pool.

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H005 — eNPS drops often precede voluntary attrition by 60-90 days

**Context:** Retrospective analysis of eNPS trends prior to voluntary departures.

**Lesson:** In most cases where a valued employee voluntarily left, their eNPS score (or equivalent engagement signal) had been declining for at least one quarter before the resignation. This means eNPS is a useful leading indicator — but only if it is acted on, not just measured. A drop in an individual's engagement score (relative to prior) should trigger a manager conversation within 2 weeks. [ASSUMPTION: This lesson is based on qualitative pattern observation, not a controlled study. The 60-90 day figure is illustrative, not statistically precise.]

**Lesson confidence:** Medium (pattern is real; timeframe is approximate)

### H006 — Offer acceptance rate below 70% always has a diagnosable cause

**Context:** Multiple hiring processes where the offer acceptance rate was below target.

**Lesson:** When offers are declined, the reasons are almost always one of five things: (1) competing offer at higher comp, (2) concerns about company stage or stability, (3) role clarity confusion that emerged during interviews, (4) candidate experience was poor (slow process, poor communication), or (5) the candidate got a better opportunity elsewhere. Nadia should conduct a post-decline interview with every candidate who declines an offer, using a structured question set. The data from these interviews is more valuable than the candidate — it tells you what is broken in your pitch, process, or comp.

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H007 — Manager communication style is the #1 predictor of early new hire retention

**Context:** 90-day review retrospective across multiple hires.

**Lesson:** New hires who reported feeling well-communicated-with by their manager in the first 30 days were significantly more likely to still be employed at 12 months. Communication clarity here means: clear role expectations, regular 1:1s, prompt feedback, and explicit acknowledgment of early contributions. The onboarding checklist (30-day check-in, documented goals) exists to structure this — but it only works if managers actually do it. Nadia should track manager compliance with onboarding check-ins and flag non-compliance to the human, not just to the manager.

**Lesson confidence:** Medium (observed across multiple hires; not formally studied)

---

### H008 — PIPs that are not preceded by documented coaching almost always fail legally and practically

**Context:** Situations where PIPs were proposed without prior documented coaching conversations.

**Lesson:** A PIP that appears suddenly — without prior coaching conversations on record — is both legally fragile and practically ineffective. Legally, it creates discrimination risk if the standard was not applied consistently. Practically, the employee has not been given a fair opportunity to improve. Nadia requires that any PIP is preceded by at least 2-3 documented coaching conversations where specific improvement expectations were communicated. "We've told them verbally" is not sufficient. Documentation must exist before the PIP is initiated. Nadia will not support a PIP without this documentation.

**Lesson confidence:** High

---

### H009 — Commission structures should be designed with HR and Finance together, not by Sales alone

**Context:** Commission plan changes that created unintended team behavior or inequitable outcomes.

**Lesson:** Commission structures designed by Sales teams alone frequently optimize for what is easy to measure rather than what the company actually wants. Common failure modes: (1) plans that reward volume at the expense of margin, (2) plans that create sales-engineering conflict by incentivizing promises that engineering cannot support, (3) plans where accelerators create an "eat what you kill" culture that damages team collaboration. Nadia should be in every commission design conversation from the start — not to approve it, but to flag the behavioral incentives the plan creates.

**Lesson confidence:** High

## Lessons to Validate

### TH001 — Span of control above 8 direct reports correlates with lower team eNPS

**Tentative lesson:** In multiple org observations, managers with more than 8 direct reports showed lower team eNPS in their reporting teams than managers with 4-6 direct reports. The hypothesis: beyond 8, managers cannot maintain 1:1 frequency and quality, which drives the engagement decline.

**Confidence:** Low (limited observations, no controlled comparison)
**Recommendation:** Track this as a hypothesis. Flag any manager above 8 direct reports for a manager effectiveness check-in.
