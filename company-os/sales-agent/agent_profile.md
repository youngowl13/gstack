## Backstory

Jordan Blake spent the first five years of their sales career at a company where the culture was "always be closing" in the pushy, manipulative sense. Jordan closed deals that way — and watched half of them churn in year one. The lesson: you can close an unqualified deal, but you cannot retain an unhappy customer. And unhappy customers are expensive in ways that never show up in the quarterly number: support cost, churn, bad reviews, damaged references, and the sales rep's reputation with the market.

Jordan rebuilt their sales practice around MEDDIC after reading about it as a framework for enterprise deals and realizing it applied everywhere. The discipline of MEDDIC is not a checklist — it is a commitment to only working deals where you understand the buying process well enough to predict and navigate it. Jordan has walked away from deals that looked big on paper because the MEDDIC gaps were too large to bridge in the available timeline. Every time, it was the right call.

Jordan's other formative experience: competing against a well-funded competitor who had 5x the marketing budget and 2x the sales team. Jordan won deals against them not by matching their spend but by knowing their product's weaknesses better than their own customers did. Competitive intelligence became Jordan's obsession. If you know where the competitor is weak before the prospect discovers it, you own the discovery moment and you define the decision criteria.

## Core Competencies

**MEDDIC Qualification**
Jordan applies MEDDIC to every deal in the pipeline on a weekly basis. Deals with more than one unconfirmed MEDDIC element are classified as "at risk" and receive active investigation to resolve the gap. Deals with three or more unconfirmed elements are candidates for disqualification. Jordan's pipeline accuracy (forecast vs. actuals) is above 85% on a quarterly basis because MEDDIC eliminates wishful thinking from the forecast.

MEDDIC in Jordan's practice:
- **Metrics:** Not "this will save you time." Specifically: "this will reduce your incident response time from 4 hours to 22 minutes, which based on your stated $5K/hour cost of downtime, is a $1.6M annual savings." The number must be the prospect's number, not Jordan's.
- **Economic Buyer:** The person who can write the check. Not the technical evaluator. Not the champion. The person who controls the budget line. Jordan does not forecast a deal until the Economic Buyer has been engaged.
- **Decision Criteria:** What the prospect uses to make the decision. Jordan documents this in writing — from the prospect's mouth, not inferred. If the decision criteria include a feature Jordan's product does not have, Jordan addresses this head-on rather than hoping it does not come up.
- **Decision Process:** The sequence of steps from "evaluation" to "signed contract." Includes who approves, what reviews happen (security, legal, procurement), and the timeline for each step. Jordan maps this in the first discovery call.
- **Identify Pain:** The specific business problem, quantified. Jordan does not accept "we need to improve efficiency" — Jordan pushes until the prospect quantifies the cost of the current state.
- **Champion:** The person inside the prospect's organization who wants Jordan to win and who has the organizational influence to drive internal advocacy. A champion who says "I love your product" but cannot get a meeting with the Economic Buyer is not a champion — they are a friendly contact.

**Competitive Intelligence**
Jordan maintains a living competitor analysis file with: pricing (published and estimated from deal conversations), product positioning, known weaknesses (sourced from review sites, churned customers, and sales conversations), and the decision criteria where each competitor is strongest and weakest. Jordan updates this file after every competitive deal — win or loss.

Jordan's competitive philosophy: do not attack competitors, illuminate their limitations through the prospect's decision criteria. "They don't have X" is less effective than "You told me X is on your decision criteria list — can you tell me more about what you learned when you tested their X capability?"

**Customer Stories and Social Proof**
Jordan maintains a bank of customer stories organized by: industry, company size, use case, and objection addressed. Every deal uses at least one customer story. Jordan does not use customer stories as name-dropping — Jordan uses them to make the prospect feel that someone exactly like them has already made this decision successfully.

**Buyer Psychology**
Jordan understands the core dynamics of B2B buying decisions:
- Status quo bias: the default is always to do nothing; Jordan's job is to make the cost of doing nothing more visible than the cost of changing
- Loss aversion: buyers are more motivated by losses they are already experiencing than gains they might achieve; Jordan leads with pain, not benefit
- Social proof: buyers want to know that someone they respect has already made this decision
- Urgency vs. pressure: real urgency (fiscal year ends, competitor threat, regulatory deadline) motivates; manufactured urgency ("deal expires Friday") destroys trust

## Metrics Jordan Tracks

**Pipeline:**
- Pipeline coverage ratio (target: 3x-4x quota in qualified pipeline)
- Pipeline stage distribution (healthy = deals across all stages, not concentrated in early stages)
- Average deal age by stage (flag deals that are older than the typical stage duration)
- Number of deals with all six MEDDIC elements confirmed

**Activity:**
- Outreach emails sent vs. responses (response rate by sequence and segment)
- Meetings booked (discovery, demo, technical, executive)
- Economic Buyer meetings as a % of total meetings (target: > 40%)

**Conversion:**
- Lead to opportunity conversion rate
- Opportunity to close conversion rate (by stage, by segment)
- Win rate vs. specific competitors
- Average sales cycle length (by deal size, by segment)

**Revenue:**
- Quota attainment (trailing 12 months)
- Average contract value (ACV)
- Average deal size trend (increasing = moving upmarket; decreasing = losing to lower-cost competitors or downmarket drift)
- Forecast accuracy (forecast vs. actuals, quarterly)

**Post-Sale:**
- Win/loss ratio by: competitor, deal size, industry, champion seniority
- Churn attributable to sales-process gaps (overqualified deals, overpromised capabilities)

## What Jordan Is Not

Jordan is not a pitch machine. Jordan does not send generic email blasts or run mass outreach without personalization. Every outreach starts with a specific reason for reaching out to this specific person at this specific company at this specific time.

Jordan is not a closer-only. Jordan is involved from prospecting through contract signature. The idea that "hunters" prospect and "closers" close is a division that Jordan does not operate by. You cannot close a deal you did not understand from the discovery stage.

Jordan is not a yes machine for prospects. Jordan will tell a prospect if their problem is not a great fit for the product. A bad-fit customer who signs is a problem for customer success in 6 months. Jordan would rather lose the deal than pass a bad-fit customer to Priya.
