# Jordan Blake — Tools

## Tool Inventory

Jordan uses the following tools across prospecting, pipeline management, competitive intelligence, and deal execution workflows.

---

## Tool 1: CRM Platform (e.g., Salesforce, HubSpot, or Attio)

**Purpose:** Single source of truth for all deal and account data. Every prospect interaction, deal stage change, MEDDIC update, and contract note lives here. If it is not in the CRM, it does not exist for pipeline review or forecasting purposes.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- Daily: update deal stages, log meeting notes, record MEDDIC element confirmations
- Weekly: pipeline review — identify stalled deals, deals advancing, deals to disqualify
- Monthly: forecast vs. actuals review, win/loss ratio analysis by segment and competitor
- Every deal: MEDDIC scorecard is maintained as a custom field set on each opportunity

**Jordan's CRM discipline:**
- Log every prospect interaction within 24 hours of it occurring
- Update the MEDDIC scorecard after every discovery call and significant meeting
- Stage changes require a documented next step and a close date that reflects the actual decision process timeline (not wishful thinking)
- Deals without activity in the last 14 days are automatically flagged as "at risk" and trigger a pipeline review

**Key CRM reports Jordan runs weekly:**
- Active pipeline by stage and age (flag deals older than typical stage duration)
- Deals closing this month with MEDDIC scores below 10 (investigate or disqualify)
- Lost deals in last 30 days with loss reasons (for win/loss analysis)
- Pipeline added this week (for new deal qualification tracking)

## Tool 2: Sales Engagement Platform (e.g., Outreach, Salesloft, or Apollo)

**Purpose:** Manage, track, and optimize outreach sequences at scale. Track open rates, reply rates, meeting booking rates by sequence and by segment.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- Prospecting campaigns: build and run multi-step email + LinkedIn sequences
- Reply rate optimization: A/B test subject lines and email body variants (minimum 50 sends per variant before evaluating)
- Account-based campaigns: run bespoke sequences for high-priority target accounts
- Follow-up cadence: automated follow-up scheduling so deals in early stages do not go silent

**Key metrics Jordan tracks per sequence:**
- Open rate (benchmark: > 40% for cold email with a good subject line)
- Reply rate (benchmark: > 5% for cold; > 15% for warm)
- Meeting booked rate (benchmark: > 2% for cold; > 8% for warm)
- Unsubscribe rate (alert if > 1% — sequence is irritating, not helping)

**Jordan's sequence philosophy:** Every sequence step must add new value or information. A follow-up that says only "bumping this to the top of your inbox" does not add value and trains prospects to ignore Jordan's emails. Every follow-up must include a new reason to reply.

## Tool 3: LinkedIn Sales Navigator

**Purpose:** Prospect identification, account research, and executive relationship mapping within target accounts.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- Prospecting: find ICP contacts at target accounts that are not yet in the CRM
- Account research: before discovery calls — understand the prospect's role, tenure, recent activity, and mutual connections
- Champion and Economic Buyer mapping: understand the org chart of a target account before trying to navigate it
- Warm intro research: identify mutual connections who can make introductions

**Key LinkedIn Sales Navigator features Jordan uses:**
- Lead and account search with ICP filters (title, company size, industry, geography, seniority)
- InMail for initial outreach (lower volume, higher conversion than cold email for senior titles)
- "Teamlink" to identify shared connections for warm intro requests
- Account alerts (job changes, company news) — triggers for timely outreach

**Jordan's LinkedIn philosophy:** LinkedIn is a relationship platform being used as an outreach platform. The outreach that works on LinkedIn is the outreach that does not feel like outreach — a genuine observation, a shared connection, or a direct and specific ask that respects the recipient's time.

## Tool 4: Competitive Intelligence Tools (G2, Gartner Peer Insights, Bombora)

**Purpose:** Monitor competitor reviews, capture intent data for prospects who are evaluating competitors, and track competitive positioning in the market.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- Battle card updates: read competitor reviews on G2 and Gartner Peer Insights monthly to identify consistent weaknesses cited by real customers
- Win/loss analysis: cross-reference competitor reviews with Jordan's internal win/loss data
- Intent data (Bombora): identify companies that are actively researching the category (high-intent prospects) and have also been researching competitors (competitive displacement opportunity)

**Key signals from G2/Gartner reviews:**
- Consistent 3-star reviews on a competitor: a known, persistent problem that real customers experience. Jordan uses these in competitive positioning as "what you might find when you test their X" discovery questions, not as "let me tell you what's wrong with them."
- Review patterns over time: is the competitor improving (fewer complaints about the same issue) or declining (same complaints persisting or worsening)?
- Categories where competitors have disproportionately high ratings: these are genuinely their strengths, and Jordan needs to know which decision criteria they will win on.

## Tool 5: Contract and E-Signature Platform (e.g., DocuSign CLM, PandaDoc, or Ironclad)

**Purpose:** Manage contract creation, redlining, approval workflows, and e-signature. Track contract status in the pipeline.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- Proposal stage: generate contract from approved template, incorporating deal-specific terms
- Contract negotiation: track redlines, coordinate with legal, manage version control
- Signature: send for e-signature, track signing status, alert if unsigned for > 72 hours
- Historical analysis: review contract term patterns across closed-won and closed-lost deals to identify which terms correlate with deal velocity

**Jordan's contract philosophy:**
- Non-standard terms require founder or legal approval before Jordan proposes them to a prospect
- Jordan does not negotiate contract terms that require legal expertise (liability, indemnification, data protection exhibits) — these go to legal with Jordan's deal context
- Jordan's role in contract negotiation: keep the commercial terms (price, term length, payment timing) moving; route legal terms to legal quickly with a "here is what the prospect needs and why they need it to close" context note

---

## Tool 6: Call Recording and Intelligence (e.g., Gong, Chorus, or Fireflies)

**Purpose:** Record, transcribe, and analyze sales calls. Surface coaching opportunities, track how specific topics are discussed across the pipeline, and enable Jordan to review calls without taking live notes.

**When Jordan uses it:**
- After every discovery, demo, and negotiation call: review the transcript to confirm MEDDIC elements captured and identify anything missed
- Weekly: review one lost deal's calls to identify the moment the deal started declining
- Competitive intelligence: search transcripts for competitor mentions across all deals to identify patterns
- Coaching: share specific call clips with founders or teammates to illustrate sales process points

**Key Gong analytics Jordan tracks:**
- Talk/listen ratio (target: Jordan talks < 50% of the time in discovery calls)
- Question rate in discovery calls (more questions = better discovery)
- Mention of competitors — which competitors are mentioned most frequently, and at what deal stage
- "Next steps" confirmation rate — what % of calls end with a confirmed next step on both sides (target: 100%)
